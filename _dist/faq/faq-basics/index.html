Basic rules FAQ

The questions every new player asks, answered precisely — merge rules, spawn rules, win conditions, and the edge cases the tutorials skip.

Merging rules

Can three tiles merge? No — merges are strictly pairwise: two equal tiles combine per swipe, per row. Can a tile merge twice in one swipe? Yes: 2-2-4-4 swiping left becomes 4-4, which merges again into 8. Each row resolves in one pass, chaining on newly formed tiles.,Do tiles merge into walls? Tiles slide until blocked; walls at board edges count as blockers. New tiles formed by a merge do not merge again in the same swipe with a tile that did not move — the resolution order is fixed: slide, merge pairs, slide once more if space remains.

Spawn rules

Every swipe spawns exactly one tile — 2 with 90% probability, 4 with 10% — in a uniformly random empty cell. No empty cells, no spawn. Spawns never stack onto existing tiles and never skip a turn.,Can I predict spawns? No — they are uniformly random and independent. You can position so that all spawn locations are equally harmless, which is the entire content of the spawn-management guide.

Winning and losing

You win at 2048 (the board celebrates and offers to continue). You lose when no cell is empty AND no two adjacent tiles match — no move can change the board. Losses are final; there is no undo.,Does continuing past 2048 change anything? Score keeps accruing at doubled values; the board does not get harder except through fullness. The continue decision is pure score expectancy (see the math guide).

Scoring

Each merge scores the merged tile's value: two 64s make 128 points. Spawns score nothing. Score is a weighted merge count — useful as effort measure, misleading as skill measure (merge rate and board state predict skill better).

Drill plan for the next ten games

Reading becomes play through repetition with attention, so here is the ten-game plan this page implies: games one through three, play normally but log the page's detection signal after each game. Games four through seven, play with the signal as an explicit mid-game checkpoint. Games eight through ten, drop the scaffolding and check only the final log. The signal should move before your score does; if it does not, the drill needs another pass with slower play.

Two environment notes for the drill window: keep the board size you are weakest on (the variants library explains why each size stresses different skills), and keep sessions under thirty minutes — the fatigue signature in the endgame guide arrives faster than players expect, and tired logs are misleading logs.

After the ten games, compare your log against the guide's expectations rather than against other players. Personal baselines differ; the trend is the teaching. If the signal moved, move to the next guide in the school's order. If it did not, the guide's failure-mode section names the usual blockers — and the FAQ's strategy group answers the questions that most often stall the drills.

More basics questions

Can two tiles merge if a third tile sits between them? No — merges are adjacency-only. The tiles must be neighbors at the moment the swipe resolves, and the merge consumes the pair, moving the result into the lead tile's position. This is why lane management matters: junk between partners breaks the adjacency that the merge requires.

What happens when the board fills completely? The game ends only when no swipe can change the board: every cell full and no equal neighbors. A full board with any mergeable pair is still alive. The endgame guide covers reading which pairs survive on a crowded board — it is the single most valuable late-game skill.

Do I get penalized for swiping into a wall? No score penalty exists, but the move still spawns a tile, and a move that changes nothing is the definition of a dead move. The economy guide counts these precisely; the practical rule is that wall-bumps are wasted turns unless they set up the next merge, which they rarely do.

Which direction should I start swiping? The direction that pushes tiles into your chosen corner — the corner-strategy guide covers the choice. The deeper answer is that the first swipe matters less than the first ten: a coherent opening builds the wall your whole game stands on, and incoherent swiping in the first ten moves is why many boards are lost before 128.
