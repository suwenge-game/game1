How to use this FAQ

Each answer below is deliberately precise rather than conversational: exact merge resolution order, exact spawn probabilities, exact persistence behavior. Precision matters because most 2048 folklore comes from players inferring rules from noisy observations — three near-simultaneous merges read as "a triple merge," a lucky 4-spawn streak read as "the game is helping me." The precise answers are also the practical ones: you cannot build a corner strategy on guessed rules.

Read order matters by experience level. New players should take the basic rules group in full — merge chaining and the spawn contract explain ninety percent of beginner surprise moments. Intermediate players usually arrive with an input or persistence question. Strategy questions (should I switch corners, is a 4 good) are answered with a pointer plus a position — the full arguments live in the strategy school and math corner, and this FAQ does not duplicate them.

If an answer here contradicts something you have read elsewhere, this site's version matches the actual game code on this site — the rules text and the implementation are maintained together, and corrections to either are published through the editorial policy's corrections process. That is the advantage of a site that writes its own game and its own tutorials: they cannot drift apart.

Putting this into practice

Knowledge from this page converts to skill through one specific drill, and the drill is named in the guide you just read. Run it today — the interval between learning and doing is where most strategy evaporates. If the drill needs a specific board state, the daily challenge's fixed seed gives you a reproducible board: replay it until the drill decision becomes automatic.

Track one number for the week after reading: the guide's detection signal (press counts, merge rate, dead-move clusters). Numbers convert vague self-assessment into a trend line, and the trend line tells you whether the guide actually moved your play. Ten games is the minimum sample — single games are noise, as the probability page explains.

Pair reading with the opposite input mode from your usual one. The friction of an unfamiliar input slows your swipes to the speed of your new knowledge, which is exactly the tempo learning needs. Once the guide's signal is visible in slow play, return to your main input and the improvement carries over.

When the advice conflicts

This site's guides occasionally overlap, and where they seem to conflict there is always a priority rule: board-state signals override structural habits, and structural habits override statistical averages. Corner strategy (structural) beats merge-rate optimization (statistical) in the early game; the order reverses past 2048 where every structure is mature and the economy is what remains.

When this site disagrees with advice you have read elsewhere, the disagreement is usually scope: our figures are computed from this site's actual answer bank and this implementation's spawn rules. Advice written for the original game's tuning may not transfer exactly — where it matters, the pages name the assumption. When in doubt, trust the page that shows its numbers and shows when they were computed.

Drill plan for the next ten games

Reading becomes play through repetition with attention, so here is the ten-game plan this page implies: games one through three, play normally but log the page's detection signal after each game. Games four through seven, play with the signal as an explicit mid-game checkpoint. Games eight through ten, drop the scaffolding and check only the final log. The signal should move before your score does; if it does not, the drill needs another pass with slower play.

Two environment notes for the drill window: keep the board size you are weakest on (the variants library explains why each size stresses different skills), and keep sessions under thirty minutes — the fatigue signature in the endgame guide arrives faster than players expect, and tired logs are misleading logs.

After the ten games, compare your log against the guide's expectations rather than against other players. Personal baselines differ; the trend is the teaching. If the signal moved, move to the next guide in the school's order. If it did not, the guide's failure-mode section names the usual blockers — and the FAQ's strategy group answers the questions that most often stall the drills.

Why this site shows its work

Every strategy claim here names its mechanism — the information-theoretic reason a corner anchor works, the arithmetic reason merge rate predicts ceilings, the probability math behind spawn positioning. Mechanisms survive changes to the numbers; slogans do not. When the site's figures update with its bank, the guides that depend on them note the change — which is why the pages carry computation dates instead of pretending to be timeless.
