What is this site?

DeskGame.xyz hosts a free browser 2048 with three board sizes, a daily challenge, and an editorial layer: strategy school, math corner, and variants library written and maintained by the site's editorial desk. The game runs entirely in your browser; no download, no account.,The game implements the classic 2048 rules with original code. It credits the original game concept; all text, tutorials, and tools on this site are original work — see the terms page for the full copyright statement.

What data does the site collect?

Game state and preferences persist in your browser's local storage; they never leave your device. Analytics load only after consent (consent mode v2 defaults to denied), and advertising loads only after ad consent. The privacy page documents the exact categories, purposes, and your withdrawal options.,The site is funded by advertising shown after consent. There are no paywalls, no loot-box mechanics, no data sales — the editorial policy and terms pages state the operational commitments in plain language.

How do I report a bug or a wrong tutorial claim?

The contact page lists channels: game support, content corrections, privacy requests, and copyright complaints, each with its own route and expected response time. Editorial corrections are published with a note on the corrected page — the corrections log is part of the editorial policy.,Bug reports that include browser version, input mode, and a description of the misfire get the fastest fixes. Tutorial corrections that include the board position and the disputed claim get the fastest review.

Can I use the tutorials elsewhere?

Site text is copyrighted; classroom and personal use of printed excerpts is fine with attribution. Republishing pages wholesale or mirroring the game is not permitted without written permission — write to the contact address; educational use is almost always approved.

Drill plan for the next ten games

Reading becomes play through repetition with attention, so here is the ten-game plan this page implies: games one through three, play normally but log the page's detection signal after each game. Games four through seven, play with the signal as an explicit mid-game checkpoint. Games eight through ten, drop the scaffolding and check only the final log. The signal should move before your score does; if it does not, the drill needs another pass with slower play.

Two environment notes for the drill window: keep the board size you are weakest on (the variants library explains why each size stresses different skills), and keep sessions under thirty minutes — the fatigue signature in the endgame guide arrives faster than players expect, and tired logs are misleading logs.

After the ten games, compare your log against the guide's expectations rather than against other players. Personal baselines differ; the trend is the teaching. If the signal moved, move to the next guide in the school's order. If it did not, the guide's failure-mode section names the usual blockers — and the FAQ's strategy group answers the questions that most often stall the drills.

Why this site shows its work

Every strategy claim here names its mechanism — the information-theoretic reason a corner anchor works, the arithmetic reason merge rate predicts ceilings, the probability math behind spawn positioning. Mechanisms survive changes to the numbers; slogans do not. When the site's figures update with its bank, the guides that depend on them note the change — which is why the pages carry computation dates instead of pretending to be timeless.

More site questions

Who writes the strategy guides? The editorial desk — the same group that maintains the game code, so guide claims and game behavior cannot silently diverge. Each guide shows its computation date where figures are cited; corrections follow the published corrections process, and the log is visible rather than quietly rewritten. Reader corrections are the second-largest source of edits after internal review.

Why three board sizes instead of more variants? Each size trains a distinct skill layer: compression (3×3), reference balance (4×4), and planning depth (5×5). Additional grid sizes add configuration without adding a new skill layer, and untested layers dilute the tutorial corpus. The variants that exist beyond sizes — speed sessions and the daily seed — were added because they train something the sizes cannot.

Can I mirror or reprint the site's content? Educational reprint of short excerpts with attribution is fine, including classroom use. Wholesale mirroring is not — the contact page handles permissions, and approval for educational use is routine. The printable materials are explicitly cleared for non-commercial printing; that is what they are for.

How is the site funded? Advertising, shown only after consent, and deliberately absent from legal pages. The funding model constrains what the site will not do — no paywalls, no data sales, no interstitials over the game board — and those constraints are written into the terms page so they are promises rather than preferences.
