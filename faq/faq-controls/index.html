Controls & modes FAQ

Input options, board sizes, session persistence, and accessibility features — the practical questions about playing on this site.

Input options

Arrow keys (desktop) and swipe (touch) are fully equivalent mechanically. WASD works as an alias. The swipe threshold is deliberately forgiving; diagonal swipes resolve to the dominant axis. See the input comparison page for ergonomics and misfire behavior.,Keyboard restart is R (with confirm). Undo does not exist by design — the game's decisions matter because they are irreversible, and the practice mode exists for experimentation.

Board sizes

3×3, 4×4, and 5×5 share rules and scoring; geometry changes strategy weightings (see the variants library). Size selection is remembered between sessions. Best scores are tracked per size, and the daily challenge is 4×4 for leaderboard comparability.,Switching sizes mid-run abandons the run — the board state belongs to its geometry. The site prompts before abandoning an active board with a score above 1,000.

Session persistence

Boards persist in local browser storage: close the tab, return, continue. Clearing site data clears boards (privacy page details what is stored — game state and settings only, no analytics without consent). No account, no sync: boards are local by design.,Incognito/private windows do not persist boards between sessions — a feature for shared machines, an inconvenience for marathons. The 5×5 marathon guide suggests planning around it.

Accessibility

The board supports keyboard-only play, screen-reader cell announcements on focus, and reduced-motion animation. Color contrast on tiles meets WCAG AA; tile values are also visually distinguished by size and position, not color alone. Gaps and bugs: the contact page routes to the editor directly.

Drill plan for the next ten games

Reading becomes play through repetition with attention, so here is the ten-game plan this page implies: games one through three, play normally but log the page's detection signal after each game. Games four through seven, play with the signal as an explicit mid-game checkpoint. Games eight through ten, drop the scaffolding and check only the final log. The signal should move before your score does; if it does not, the drill needs another pass with slower play.

Two environment notes for the drill window: keep the board size you are weakest on (the variants library explains why each size stresses different skills), and keep sessions under thirty minutes — the fatigue signature in the endgame guide arrives faster than players expect, and tired logs are misleading logs.

After the ten games, compare your log against the guide's expectations rather than against other players. Personal baselines differ; the trend is the teaching. If the signal moved, move to the next guide in the school's order. If it did not, the guide's failure-mode section names the usual blockers — and the FAQ's strategy group answers the questions that most often stall the drills.

Why this site shows its work

Every strategy claim here names its mechanism — the information-theoretic reason a corner anchor works, the arithmetic reason merge rate predicts ceilings, the probability math behind spawn positioning. Mechanisms survive changes to the numbers; slogans do not. When the site's figures update with its bank, the guides that depend on them note the change — which is why the pages carry computation dates instead of pretending to be timeless.

More controls questions

Can I play one-handed on a phone? Yes — the touch layer accepts thumb-arc swipes from anywhere on the board, and the board's layout keeps all merge-relevant feedback within the thumb's arc. Landscape orientation works but changes the swipe geometry; players who rotate mid-game should re-check their direction mapping on the first swipe after rotating.

Why does the board sometimes not register a swipe? The two common causes are a swipe that ended outside the board's gesture area and a swipe during the animation lockout window. Both are visible: the board state is unchanged, so the move was not consumed. The registration rule is deliberately conservative to prevent accidental double-moves; slow the gesture slightly and the input lands.

Does the site work offline? After the first load, the game runs from your browser cache and continues to work through brief connection drops; scores persist locally and sync nothing (there is nothing to sync — the game is accountless by design). The daily challenge requires a connection to verify the date, but practice mode is fully offline.

Is there an undo? No, by design — irreversibility is what makes direction discipline a skill rather than a preference. The practice-mode pattern (replay a seed with a different opening) covers the experimentation need that undo usually fills, without corrupting the main game's decision weight.
